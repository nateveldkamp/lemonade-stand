🍋 Why this works the way it does

This is a course in two things at once: how to build and run a business, and how to do that work hand-in-hand with AI. They're taught together, on purpose — because that's how the work actually happens now.

Business and AI, as one skill

Most training treats AI as a separate subject — a tool you bolt onto what you already do. We don't. The business skills (pricing, accounting, operations, marketing, strategy) and the AI skills (giving good context, organizing your work, automating it, judging what comes back) are inseparable in real life, so we never separate them. From your first day you're doing real work with an AI, not learning about AI on the side.

Why a lemonade stand

A lemonade stand is the simplest complete business anyone can picture. On day one — just a card table and a pitcher — you already do the whole job: decide what to charge, get people to buy, make sure you don't run out, and check whether you actually made money. That's the entire game of business, just small.

And it scales. The same stand can grow into a chain, a brand, a manufacturer, a global public company. So one familiar story can carry you from the very basics all the way to boardroom-level complexity — adding one new layer at a time, in an order that always makes sense.

Playing the whole game

This design follows an idea from Harvard education researcher David Perkins, in his book Making Learning Whole. Perkins noticed that we usually teach the elements of something — the grammar, the scales, the formulas — for years before we ever let someone play the actual game.

It's as if children had to practice batting and catching and running bases for a couple of years before ever playing baseball — most would lose interest long before the game began, and never understand why the drills mattered.

His alternative: play the whole game from the start — a "junior version" that's simplified but still recognizably the real thing. You meet the hard parts inside a game you already care about, which is what makes them worth learning.

The lemonade stand is our junior version of the whole game. From the first day you run a real (tiny) business end to end. Then we add complexity a layer at a time as the stand grows — never drilling a skill in isolation, always in service of the game you're already playing.

Why this matters now

AI has changed software engineering faster than any other kind of work — not because coders are special, but because their work already lives in one organized, version-tracked place (a "repo") that an AI can fully see and work in. The context is structured and reviewable.

Most other knowledge work isn't like that. The context lives scattered across inboxes, slide decks, spreadsheets, SaaS tools, and people's heads. Closing that gap — bringing that same structure to the rest of knowledge work — is where the next wave of AI value is, and it's a real opportunity for the people who learn how.

The deliverable is shifting, too: less the individual report, analysis, or plan, and more the "factory" — the system that produces those on demand, from organized context. The skills that build that factory — organizing your work, versioning it, automating it, and judging AI's output — are exactly what this course teaches. You even finish by helping improve this very project: a living, version-controlled system, the same kind you'll build for your own work.
